# Do I Need an Emergency Electrician or Can It Wait?

When you notice something wrong with your home’s electrics, the first question is usually: do I call someone now, or can it wait until normal hours? The short answer is that any issue posing an immediate risk of fire, electric shock, or serious damage is an emergency. Everything else can likely wait for a scheduled appointment. This article helps you tell the difference, so you can act quickly, stay safe, and avoid unnecessary call-out costs.

---

## What Counts as an Electrical Emergency in the UK?

An electrical emergency is any fault that puts people or property at immediate risk. UK regulations (BS 7671:2018, the 18th Edition of the Wiring Regulations) set the safety standards for domestic electrical installations. When those standards are breached in a way that could cause harm, you need to act fast.

The following warning signs mean you should treat the situation as an emergency:

- **Burning smell, smoke, or visible flames** from an outlet, switch, or consumer unit (fuse box). Overheating wires or arcing can ignite nearby materials.
- **Sparks or arcing** when you plug something in or operate a switch. Occasional small sparks on plugging in can be normal, but repeated or large sparks indicate a fault.
- **Electric shock** when touching an appliance, switch, or outlet. Even a mild tingle can signal unsafe wiring.
- **Exposed live wires** – for example, damage to a cable that reveals copper conductors.
- **Hot outlets, switches, or wires** that are warm to the touch even when nothing is drawing power.
- **Frequent circuit breaker tripping** that you cannot reset (or that trips again immediately). This points to a short circuit or earth fault.
- **Partial power loss** with no tripped breakers – this may indicate a loose connection or damaged neutral wire, which can cause voltage surges and fire risk.
- **Water or flooding near electrical systems** – do not step into water if electrics are live. Call emergency services.

For any of these, the correct first step is to isolate the affected circuit if it is safe to do so, then call an emergency electrician or, if there is immediate danger to life, call 999.

## Electrical Issues That Can Usually Wait Until Morning

Some problems are inconvenient but not dangerous. If none of the above signs are present, you can normally wait for a standard daytime appointment.

- **Single outlet not working** – especially if it is an older socket or one that has never been used. Check if a nearby switch (like a fused connection unit) has tripped.
- **Light bulb failure** – a single bulb or fitting not working is rarely urgent. Check the bulb, switch, and any local switching.
- **Flickering lights** – if they flicker only when you switch on a high-power appliance, the cause may be a loose connection but it is usually not an immediate danger. Monitor it and book an inspection if it persists.
- **Area power cut** – if power goes out across several homes, call your local Distribution Network Operator (DNO) on **105** (free in England, Scotland, and Wales). This is not an emergency unless you suspect a fault in your own wiring. Do not call an electrician for an area outage.
- **Appliance not working** – if a single device fails and there are no other signs, try plugging it into another circuit. If it still doesn’t work, the problem is likely with the appliance, not your wiring.

Always keep an eye on non-urgent faults. If they change or escalate, treat them as an emergency.

## What to Do When You Have an Electrical Emergency

If you confirm an electrical emergency, follow these steps to protect yourself, your family, and your home.

**Step 1: If there is a risk of fire, electric shock, or someone has been hurt, call 999.** Ask for the fire service (if there is fire or smoke) or an ambulance (if someone has received a shock). Do not touch a person who is still in contact with live electricity. Switch off the mains supply first, or use a non-conductive object like a wooden broom handle to push them away.

**Step 2: Isolate the supply.** If it is safe, go to your consumer unit and switch off the main switch. This cuts power to the whole house. If you know which circuit is faulty, you can switch off only that breaker. Never touch the unit if you have wet hands or are standing on a damp floor.

**Step 3: Call a qualified emergency electrician.** In the UK, any work on fixed wiring should be done by a registered competent person (for example, a member of NICEIC, NAPIT, or SELECT in Scotland). Emergency electricians are available 24/7 and can often talk you through safe steps over the phone.

**Step 4: Wait somewhere safe.** If you have turned off the mains, do not try to restore power yourself. Stay clear of the affected area, especially if there is visible damage or water.

For power cuts, use 105 to reach your DNO. They will tell you if it is a network fault and when power is likely to be restored.

## Landlords, Tenants, and Legal Responsibilities

If you live in a rented property, different rules apply. The **Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020** (and similar rules in Scotland and Wales) require landlords to meet electrical safety standards set out in BS 7671. This includes arranging an Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) at least every five years and completing any remedial work identified as “C1” (danger) or “C2” (potentially dangerous) immediately.

### For tenants

- Report any electrical fault to your landlord or letting agent as soon as you notice it.
- If the fault is dangerous (sparks, burning smell, electric shock risk), that is an emergency. Treat it as such – call 999 if needed. The landlord must arrange emergency repairs, but you should not wait if lives are at risk.
- For non-urgent issues like a broken socket or flickering light, the landlord has a reasonable time to fix it. However, the regulations say they must act on C1 and C2 risks from the EICR “as soon as practicable”.

### For landlords

- Ensure your properties have a valid EICR. If a tenant reports an urgent problem, arrange an emergency electrician within a timescale that prevents danger.
- Keep records of all inspections and remedial work. Failure to comply can lead to enforcement action and fines of up to £30,000 per breach.

## When to Call an Emergency Electrician vs. a Standard Appointment

Call-out charges for emergency electricians are higher than standard daytime rates due to after-hours premiums. Typical costs vary widely, but you can expect a fee for the first hour of work plus travel. Many firms offer a free phone assessment to decide if an emergency visit is really needed.

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Burning smell, sparks, shock, or fire risk | Call emergency electrician immediately (and 999 if life-threatening) |
| Frequent trips, partial power loss, hot outlets | Call emergency electrician – fault likely needs urgent repair |
| Single broken socket, flickering lights, area power cut | Do not need emergency call – schedule standard appointment or call DNO for area outage |
| Wanted to check non-urgent wiring condition | Book an EICR during normal hours |

If you are unsure, err on the side of caution. A phone call to an emergency electrician can confirm whether you need a visit now or can wait.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What exactly counts as an electrical emergency?**  
Any situation where there is an immediate risk of fire, electric shock, or injury. Signs include burning smells, sparks, smoke, exposed live wires, electric shocks, or breakers that trip repeatedly and won’t stay reset.

**Can I fix an electrical problem myself?**  
No. In the UK, DIY electrical work on mains circuits is dangerous and often illegal. Only qualified, registered electricians should work on fixed wiring. Incorrect repairs can cause fires and invalidate insurance.

**How do I know if a circuit breaker tripping is an emergency?**  
If the breaker trips again immediately after you reset it, or trips several times a day, it indicates a fault that needs professional diagnosis. It is an emergency if the tripping is accompanied by heat, smell, or sparks.

**Should I call the electricity company for a power cut?**  
Yes. If the power is off in multiple homes, call **105** for your local distribution network operator. They handle supply faults. Only call an electrician if the power cut seems to be only in your property and you suspect a wiring fault.

**How do I find a reliable emergency electrician?**  
Look for a registered electrician who is a member of a competent person scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT. Many local services offer 24/7 emergency call-outs.
