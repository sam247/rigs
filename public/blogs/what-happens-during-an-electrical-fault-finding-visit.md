# What Happens During an Electrical Fault Finding Visit?

### What Is an Electrical Fault Finding Visit?

An electrical fault finding visit is a structured, safety-first diagnostic process carried out by a qualified electrician to identify why part of your home’s electrical installation is not working correctly or safely. The aim is to locate the root cause of symptoms such as tripping breakers, flickering lights, dead sockets, burning smells, or unexplained power loss. The visit typically includes safe isolation of the affected circuit, visual inspection, a series of targeted tests using calibrated equipment, and a clear report of findings with recommended remedial actions.

Fault finding is not guesswork. It follows a methodical, evidence-based approach aligned with the 18th edition of the Wiring Regulations (BS 7671) – the UK standard for electrical installations. Whether you are a homeowner dealing with a recurring trip or a landlord meeting your legal duties under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, a professional fault finding visit is the first step to restoring safety and function.

### When Do You Need an Electrical Fault Finding Visit?

You should arrange a fault finding visit as soon as you notice any of these warning signs:

*   The same circuit breaker or RCD trips repeatedly.
*   Lights flicker or dim for no obvious reason.
*   Sockets, switches, or light fittings feel warm to the touch — see [signs a socket is unsafe](/blog/signs-a-socket-is-unsafe).
*   You smell burning or see scorch marks near electrical points.
*   An appliance stops working while others on the same circuit remain fine.
*   There is a buzzing or crackling sound from a socket or switch.
*   Power is lost in part of your home but the main fuse is still on.

Some faults are intermittent, making them harder to trace. A skilled electrician uses diagnostic tools such as insulation resistance testers, earth fault loop impedance meters, and thermal imaging cameras to locate hidden problems that a visual check alone cannot find.

If you are a private landlord in England, you already have a legal duty to ensure electrical installations are safe. The 2020 Regulations require an Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) at least every five years, but you must act sooner if a tenant reports a fault. Delaying can lead to enforcement action, financial penalties, and increased risk to occupants.

### The Step-by-Step Process During a Fault Finding Visit

While every job is different, a typical fault finding visit follows a recognised sequence. The electrician will:

**1. Initial triage and safety briefing**  
They start by discussing symptoms with you: what happened, when, and whether any work was done recently. This helps narrow down likely causes before any testing begins.

**2. Safe isolation**  
Before working on any circuit, the electrician isolates the affected part of the installation – often by switching off the relevant circuit breaker at the consumer unit. This prevents electric shock and allows safe access to terminals, cables, and components.

**3. Visual inspection**  
A thorough visual check covers the consumer unit, sockets, switches, light fittings, junction boxes, and visible wiring. The electrician looks for signs of overheating (melted plastic, discolouration), loose connections, damaged insulation, water ingress, or vermin activity.

**4. Instrument testing**  
Using calibrated test meters, the electrician performs a series of measurements:

*   **Continuity testing** – checks that conductors (live, neutral, earth) form a complete loop with no breaks.
*   **Insulation resistance testing** – applies a low voltage to measure the condition of cable insulation. Low readings indicate deterioration or moisture.
*   **Earth fault loop impedance** – verifies that the earth path is low enough to allow protective devices to trip quickly in a fault.
*   **Polarity checks** – confirm conductors are connected correctly at all outlets.
*   **RCD/RCBO testing** – checks that residual current devices trip within the required time and current thresholds.

**5. Fault localisation**  
By systematically eliminating healthy sections of the circuit, the electrician pinpoints the exact location of the fault. This may involve disconnecting sections of a ring main or testing each accessory individually.

**6. Diagnosis and reporting**  
Once the fault is found, the electrician categorises it:

*   **Dangerous** – immediate risk of electric shock or fire (requires urgent repair).
*   **Urgent** – fault likely to lead to a dangerous condition if left.
*   **Non-urgent** – does not present immediate danger but should be corrected.

They explain the findings to you in plain language, often supported by photos or test results. If the fault was caused by a failed accessory (e.g. a socket or light switch), they may replace it on the spot if they carry the correct part. For more complex issues, you will receive a written quote for remedial work.

**7. Re-energisation and safety check**  
After any necessary repairs, the electrician re-energises the circuit and retests to confirm the fault has been resolved and the installation remains safe. They also ensure other unaffected circuits are restored.

**8. Certification (where required)**  
If any fixed wiring has been altered, a **Minor Electrical Installation Works Certificate** is issued. If the visit was part of a periodic inspection, an updated EICR is provided. Landlords must keep these documents as evidence of compliance.

### Standards, Regulations, and Who Carries Out the Work

Not every electrician is qualified to carry out fault finding and issue the required certificates. In England, work on fixed electrical installations must meet the requirements of BS 7671 (the Wiring Regulations). For fault finding visits, you should use a **qualified person** – typically a registered electrician with a competent person scheme such as NICEIC, NAPIT, or ELECSA. These bodies require regular assessment and insurance that covers inspection, testing, and certification.

The **Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020** explicitly require that inspections and remedial work be carried out by a qualified person. For landlords, this means using an electrician who can produce a valid EICR and provide the necessary safety paperwork. Local housing authorities have enforcement powers, including the ability to issue remedial notices and carry out work at the landlord’s cost if they fail to comply.

For homeowners, while the 2020 Regulations do not directly apply, the same safety standards are strongly recommended. Mortgage lenders and insurers may require evidence of safe electrical installations, particularly after major works or a property purchase.

### What Typical Costs and Timeframes Look Like

Exact costs vary depending on your location, the complexity of the fault, and the electrician’s rates. Based on typical UK 2025 guidance, you can expect:

*   **Call-out / minimum charge**: £70 – £140 (inc. VAT)
*   **Labour**: £50 – £85 per hour
*   **Materials**: £15 – £150 (if a part is needed, e.g. a new socket, breaker, or light fitting)
*   **Total for most jobs**: £180 – £400

Time on site often ranges from 45–90 minutes for a simple socket or lighting fault, up to 2–4 hours for a ring-main issue or a complex intermittent fault. The electrician will usually give you a fixed quote after the diagnosis, so you can decide whether to proceed before any repair work starts.

To avoid surprises, ask these questions when booking:

*   Are you registered with NICEIC, NAPIT, or ELECSA and insured to issue certificates?
*   Does your quote include materials, parking, and VAT?
*   Do you carry common breakers, RCDs, and accessories so you can fix minor faults on the same visit?
*   What is your policy if the fault returns after the repair?

### How to Prepare for a Fault Finding Visit

You can help the process run smoothly:

*   **Note the symptoms** – which circuit or appliance is affected, when it started, and any pattern (e.g. only when it rains).
*   **Clear access** – move furniture away from the consumer unit and any sockets or switches the electrician will need to inspect.
*   **Send photos in advance** – if you know how, take pictures of the consumer unit and the affected fitting when you book. This lets the electrician bring the right test gear and spares.
*   **Tell them about any recent work** – including DIY, extension builds, or new appliances that may have disturbed wiring.
*   **Do not attempt repairs yourself** – fault finding requires safe isolation and specialist tools. Interfering with live circuits is dangerous and could invalidate your insurance.

If you are a landlord arranging a visit for a rented property, give the tenant at least 24 hours’ notice (unless it is an emergency) and provide written confirmation of the appointment. The tenant must allow reasonable access for inspections and repairs.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**How urgent is an electrical fault?**  
Any fault that causes a circuit to trip, sparks, or a burning smell is urgent. Turn off the affected circuit at the consumer unit and call a qualified electrician immediately. Intermittent faults are still potentially dangerous and should be investigated promptly.

**Will I need an EICR after a fault finding visit?**  
Not always. If the fault is isolated to a single accessory and the rest of the installation is sound, a Minor Works Certificate may suffice. If the visit reveals wider issues – such as poor insulation readings or an outdated consumer unit – the electrician may recommend a full EICR.

**Can I try to fix an electrical fault myself?**  
No. UK electrical work is regulated, and DIY fault finding is dangerous. You risk electric shock, fire, and invalidating your home insurance. Always use a registered, competent electrician.

**What if the fault is intermittent and the electrician cannot find it on the first visit?**  
Skilled electricians use data logging and load monitoring to capture intermittent events. If the fault does not show during the initial tests, they may set up monitoring equipment or arrange a follow-up visit when the conditions are more likely to occur. This is part of normal fault-finding practice.

**Do I need to be home during the visit?**  
For safety and to discuss symptoms, it is best if you are present – or at least ensure the electrician has clear access to the consumer unit and all rooms. If you cannot be there, provide a detailed description of the problem and leave a contact number.

**What happens if the fault is in a rented property?**  
The landlord is responsible for arranging and paying for the repair. The electrician will issue the necessary certificate, and the landlord must provide a copy to the tenant and, if requested, to the local housing authority. Failure to act can lead to enforcement.

### Next Steps

If you are experiencing any of the warning signs described above, do not ignore them. Contact a qualified electrician to arrange a fault finding visit. Many offer a free, no-obligation quote over the phone and can often attend the same day for urgent problems. After diagnosis, you will have a clear picture of what needs to be fixed, the cost, and the certificate required.

Whether you are dealing with a single tripping socket or a more complex circuit issue, a professional fault finding visit gives you confidence that your home’s electrical system is safe, compliant, and reliable.
